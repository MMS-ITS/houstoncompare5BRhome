## Four questions appear in every letter

1. **The parcel's actual tax rate** by taxing unit, including any MUD, PID or
   SUD — today and at district build-out. Largest single unknown in the model:
   on a $290,000 house, 2.2% against 3.0% is about $190/month.

2. **A bindable insurance quote**, not an estimate, with wind, hail and
   named-storm deductibles spelled out. A $2,000 error is $167/month.

3. **Whether the HOA permits letting at all** — minimum lease term, cap on the
   proportion of rented homes, waiting period after closing. Asked *before*
   anything else, because a community that forbids rentals makes its economics
   irrelevant.

4. **Whether the advertised incentive is available to an investor.** Two
   builders here advertise headline rates tied to FHA, RD or VA financing —
   none of which an investment purchase can use. Trophy Signature advertises
   3.25% (6.080% APR) with free appliances at Lakehaven; DSLD advertises 3.99%
   initial (6.788% APR) with up to $12,000 at Lake Charles, explicitly on
   FHA/RD/VA. Each letter asks for the investor-eligible equivalent by name.

## The letters state the deficit openly

Each email tells the builder or agent what the model shows and asks whether
better information changes it. That is deliberate:

- it invites a serious counter-proposal rather than a brochure;
- it signals that an incentive would have to be substantial to matter;
- on the one resale it opens the price conversation honestly, instead of
  spending two rounds getting there.
